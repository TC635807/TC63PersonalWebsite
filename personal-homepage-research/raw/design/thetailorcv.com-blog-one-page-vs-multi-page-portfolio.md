#One-Page Portfolios
Best for: early-career candidates, generalists, and anyone with 3-5 projects that do not need deep individual write-ups.
Pros:
- Fast to scan - a recruiter sees everything in one scroll
- Simple to build and maintain
- Works well on mobile with minimal navigation
Cons:
- Limited room for deep case studies - each project usually gets a short blurb, not a full narrative
- Can feel cramped once you have more than 4-5 strong projects
- Harder to target different audiences (e.g., a recruiter vs. a technical interviewer) with different depth levels
#Multi-Page Portfolios
Best for: designers and developers with detailed case studies, senior professionals with a large body of work, or anyone using How to Write a Portfolio Case Study-style deep dives.
Pros:
- Each project gets its own page with full context (Problem -> Process -> Solution -> Outcome)
- Easier to organize by category (case studies, writing samples, code repos) for different audiences
- Scales better as your body of work grows over a multi-year career
Cons:
- Takes longer to build and keep updated
- Risk of losing a recruiter's attention if navigation is unclear or projects are buried too many clicks deep
- Requires more discipline to keep every sub-page current
#How to Decide
- Count your strongest projects. Three to five shallow entries: one-page. Two to four deep case studies: multi-page.
- Consider your audience. Recruiters skim; hiring managers and technical interviewers often want depth. A hybrid - one-page overview with links out to a few deep-dive pages - serves both.
- Match your industry. UX and product design almost always benefit from multi-page case studies; sales, marketing generalists, and early-career candidates usually do better with a tight one-page summary.
#The Hybrid Approach (Usually the Right Answer)
Most strong portfolios are actually a hybrid: a single homepage with your intro, skills, and a card for each project, where 2-3 of the strongest projects link out to a dedicated deep-dive page. This gives a recruiter the fast scan and gives an interested hiring manager somewhere to go deeper. The TailorCV portfolio builder supports this structure natively across its themes.

#Real-World Scenarios: When to Use Each Portfolio Type
Understanding when to use a one-page or multi-page portfolio can often be the difference between landing a job or missing out. Here are a few scenarios to clarify when each type excels:
#Scenario 1: The Creative Designer
Situation: A graphic designer applying for a position at a creative agency. Recommendation: A one-page portfolio is ideal here. It allows the designer to showcase their best work upfront, with strong visuals that grab attention quickly. They can highlight key projects with brief descriptions, leading to a more engaging and memorable presentation.
#Scenario 2: The Software Developer
Situation: A software developer looking to demonstrate various coding projects. Recommendation: A multi-page portfolio works best. The developer can create separate pages for different projects, detailing the technologies used, challenges faced, and the impact of their work. This structure allows for more in-depth explanations, showcasing their problem-solving skills and technical expertise.
#Scenario 3: The Business Analyst
Situation: A business analyst aiming to apply for a role in a data-driven organization. Recommendation: A hybrid approach would be beneficial. The first page can summarize key skills and notable achievements, while subsequent pages delve into case studies or specific projects. This format allows the analyst to present a concise overview while still providing detailed insights when necessary. For more on this, check out our Business Analyst Portfolio Guide.

#Dos and Don'ts for Your Portfolio
When creating your portfolio, adhering to certain dos and don'ts can significantly impact its effectiveness.
Do
- Do Tailor Your Portfolio: Customize your portfolio for different job applications or target audiences.
- Do Use High-Quality Images: Visuals speak louder than words; ensure your project images are clear and professional.
- Do Keep It Updated: Regularly refresh your portfolio with new projects and skills to reflect your current capabilities.
Don't
- Don't Overload with Information: Avoid clutter; too much content can overwhelm visitors.
- Don't Use Jargon: Keep descriptions simple and relatable, especially for non-technical audiences.
- Don't Neglect Mobile Optimization: Ensure your portfolio is responsive and looks good on all devices.
